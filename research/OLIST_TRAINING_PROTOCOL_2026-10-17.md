# Proposed Olist completed-order lateness protocol — 17 October 2026

**Status: proposed, count-verified protocol; not supervisor approval, training, or a completed evaluation.** Protocol ID: `olist-native-calendar-lateness-1.0`. The new local feasibility artifacts contain aggregate counts only. No model fitting, model scoring, final row-level split/target export, download, app feature, inference activation or production action was performed.

This is a separate native-source **binary lateness conditional on recorded completion** experiment using the already acquired original Olist version 2 release. It preserves Random Forest (RF), Decision Tree (DT), Logistic Regression (LR) and a Dummy diagnostic. It neither appends Olist to DataCo nor applies the existing DataCo model as an external test: schemas, prediction times and outcome definitions differ.

Choong's original report requires Low/Medium/High delivery risk and RF/DT/LR comparisons, with a proposed 70/15/15 split. Olist lacks the complete attempt, proof and terminal-failure histories for that primary task. The report-alignment evidence is [the labelling review](LABELLING_REVIEW_2026-10-17.md); the proposed operational rubric remains distinct from report quotations in [data collection](DATA_COLLECTION.md). This binary target, purchase-time cutoff, date convention, maturity window and temporal proportions are new methodological proposals, not approved report requirements. They do not fulfil the three-class primary evaluation. The existing [34-fit DataCo/backorder development plan](TABULAR_IMPROVEMENT_PLAN_2026-10-17.md), its frozen data and its exposed-test restrictions remain unchanged.

## Prediction time, target and eligible population

Prediction time is the source `order_purchase_timestamp`. Preserve naive source timestamps: no timezone is supplied, so do not invent UTC conversion. Let `P` be purchase, `D` actual customer delivery and `E` the estimated delivery timestamp. Require unique order ID, final source status exactly `delivered`, parseable nonmissing `P,D,E`, `D >= P`, and `date(E) >= date(P)`. This reproduces the audit core: 96,470 of 99,441 orders. Keep the 2,963 non-Delivered orders and eight Delivered orders without a delivery timestamp excluded with reasons. A cancellation, unresolved shipment or missing outcome is never assigned either class, including when an estimated deadline has passed.

Primary label: `late = 1` iff `date(D) > date(E)`; otherwise `0`. All 99,441 estimates are midnight and the audited dictionary describes the estimate as the date communicated at purchase. Treating its whole calendar day as available is therefore the proposed conservative interpretation. Same-day delivery at 23:59:59 is on time; next-day 00:00:00 is late. This is an interpretation, not proof of the publisher's operational deadline. Preserve a separate strict-midnight sensitivity `late_exact = 1[D > E]`; never choose between definitions using scores. In the full core, 6,534 calendar-late versus 7,826 exact-late orders differ in 1,292 cases.

Primary eligibility does not require optional approval/carrier events. The separate chronology sensitivity excludes any present approval before purchase, carrier before purchase/approval, or delivery before purchase/approval/carrier; estimated date before purchase is already excluded by core eligibility. Missing optional dates remain unknown and do not fail that sensitivity. This reproduces the audit's 95,097-order strict cohort before temporal restrictions. Optional events and final status are for outcome/eligibility audit only, never predictors. These retrospective exclusions condition the population on future recorded completion and can introduce selection bias; neither cohort estimates all-order failure risk.

## As-of knowledge, maturity and temporal partitions

For each window use the fixed outcome cutoff `C` in the table. Retain only eligible core orders with **`D < C` and `midnight(date(E)) + 31 days <= C`**. The latter is the end-exclusive boundary of the promised calendar day plus 30 full days. Apply it equally to early and late completers; an early completion does not bypass maturity. The 30 days is an administrative observation allowance, not a late-label tolerance or evidence that unresolved orders failed. Delivery may occur more than 30 days after the deadline and is retained if recorded before `C`. At `D == C` the outcome is unavailable. At `E + 31 days == C` the maturity check passes. All purchase windows are half open.

The source is a single final snapshot with no extraction-coverage guarantee, status-event log, revision history or ingestion timestamp. `D < C` is a **necessary event-time proxy** for known completion, not proof that its row/status was available in a live system by `C`. The protocol assumes delivery was recorded contemporaneously and the purchase estimate did not change; these assumptions are unverifiable here. Never use the 2021 dataset update date or the maximum recorded date as proof of complete follow-up. The final cutoff is deliberately inside the observed delivery range (which extends to 17 October 2018), but that also cannot establish full ascertainment. The result must be described as retrospective temporal evaluation with as-of proxies; a true deployment replay requires versioned event records.

## Entity policy and one-order weight

Each order receives exactly one split and one unit of evaluation weight. Assert orders/customer-per-order keys unique; customer lookup is one-to-one by `customer_id`, resolving `customer_unique_id` for grouping. Item keys `(order_id, order_item_id)` are unique. Any audited item/product join must validate many-to-one product lookup, then aggregate **before** joining to orders; assert the output has one row per eligible order and exactly the same order set. A raw many-item join must never multiply target rows or sample weight. Dry-run joined item counts are 36,765 / 15,342 / 14,814; their order counts remain 32,245 / 13,445 / 12,842. ## Conservative purchase-time feature boundary

The **exact primary allowlist contains four source-derived variables**:

| Variable | Deterministic definition | Handling |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `purchase_month` | Month 1–12 from `P` | Categorical |
| `purchase_weekday` | Monday=0 through Sunday=6 from `P` | Categorical |
| `purchase_hour` | Integer hour 0–23 from `P` | Categorical |
| `promised_calendar_lead_days` | `date(E) - date(P)`, integer days | Numeric; zero valid |

These are native benchmark inputs, not new IILAS application features. `E` is allowed only under the audited publisher meaning that the estimate was communicated at purchase; lack of estimate revision history remains a limitation. Reject missing/nonfinite/out-of-range derived values; no imputation is needed for this complete four-variable cohort. Retain valid extreme lead times unchanged and report ranges; do not select clipping bounds using validation or confirmation. Do not add absolute year/date trend, location or other predictors after inspecting scores.

Exclude actual delivery, final status, actual duration, lateness, approval/carrier times, reviews, payments, customer/order/product/seller identifiers and `shipping_limit_date`. The last is a seller shipping field with historical values extending to 2020, not the customer arrival promise. Also exclude price, freight, item/product counts, categories, physical/descriptive product fields from **this primary experiment**: the snapshot does not verify when those values were finalised or which product version existed at purchase. This deliberately small baseline can be weaker than a richer model; temporal availability takes precedence. Item aggregates in the dry run establish grain and exposure only. Any later richer item/product variant needs a new explicitly versioned feature-provenance decision, availability assumptions, aggregation formulas and fit budget; it is not authorised implicitly by this protocol.

Deterministic extraction may run on all rows for integrity/counting, but category discovery and every learned transform fit only on the retained train cohort. Use a scikit-learn Pipeline/ColumnTransformer: `OneHotEncoder(handle_unknown='ignore')` on the three categorical variables, numeric passthrough for RF/DT, and train-fitted `StandardScaler` on the single numeric column for LR. Record unseen-category counts at validation/confirmation. No feature selection, target encoding, resampling, synthetic rows, calibration or global preprocessing fit. Freeze the encoder with its estimator; never fit an encoder separately on validation. This follows [scikit-learn's leakage guidance](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/common_pitfalls.html).

For each RF/DT/LR candidate evaluate thresholds `k/200` for integers `k=0..200` plus `nextafter(1.0,+infinity)` (202 choices). Predict 1 iff the probability for class label 1 is `>= threshold`. If all candidates fail to improve on Dummy, report that outcome without extending the grid. Good macro F1 is not operational acceptance, and threshold tuning cannot improve AP.

## Reporting and uncertainty

For every frozen family choice and Dummy report confusion matrix with true/predicted order `[0,1]`, both classes' precision/recall/F1/support, macro F1, balanced accuracy, accuracy, AP, positive prevalence, alert count/rate, false positives and false negatives, at selected threshold and 0.5. State each denominator. Provide purchase-month metrics and counts to expose temporal shift; calendar-late prevalence here changes materially between windows. Report main and sensitivity populations separately. For chronology comparisons, additionally restrict main predictions to the same retained evaluation orders to distinguish population changes from changed fitted models. For exact-label sensitivity, score the original frozen primary predictions against exact labels on the same orders as a reference; do not present calendar-label and exact-label metrics as directly interchangeable.

Use two **separate paired descriptive uncertainty analyses**, each 500 replicates with RNG seed 20261017 and fixed choices/no refitting: (1) sample unique `customer_unique_id` clusters with replacement, retaining all orders of each sampled customer and its multiplicity; (2) sample Monday-start purchase-week blocks with replacement, retaining all orders in each block. Use identical multiplicities for every model compared within each analysis. Report 2.5th/97.5th percentile intervals for macro F1, positive precision/recall, balanced accuracy and alert rate, plus paired RF-minus-DT and RF-minus-LR differences. AP stays a point estimate in this bounded protocol. Do not bootstrap individual orders as though repeat customers were independent.

For an input or replicate missing a class, flag the affected metrics unavailable; record the invalid-replicate count and never redraw until favourable/class-complete. If fewer than 475 valid replicates remain, suppress that interval and explain why. Count no-alert replicates and flag their precision convention. These two interval families do not jointly correct customer and time dependence; weekly resampling can split a recurrent customer's weeks, and customer resampling can ignore marketplace shocks. The short confirmation span has few time blocks. Neither method accounts for fitting/selection uncertainty or proves significance, calibration, causality or future prevalence stability. Validation intervals additionally condition on selection using the same validation data. Report both analyses and limitations rather than choosing the narrower interval.

Required future report flags: `protocol_status=proposed` until genuine review is recorded; `primary_iilas_task_evaluated=false`; `operational_acceptance=false`; `source_aggregate_outcomes_previously_observed=true`; `confirmation_partition_counts_previously_observed=true`; `olist_model_scores_observed_before_protocol=false`; and actual fitting/scoring phase flags. Source/split/feature integrity violations stop before fitting. Missing classes, altered source bytes, failed joins, unexpected chronology/feature range failures or incomplete output reconstruction prohibit a completion claim. Preserve honest failure records locally.
