# Olist purchase-time feature availability audit — 20 October 2026

**Decision:** richer Olist predictors are feasible candidates for a separately versioned, explicitly conditional retrospective experiment. This audit does not prove that the released values were historically available at purchase and does not authorize fitting. The narrowest useful candidate adds item count, price/freight totals and coarse seller/customer geography to the existing four features. Product attributes, distance and seller history need further gates before inclusion. No improvement in predictive performance is established.

The frozen [17 October protocol](OLIST_TRAINING_PROTOCOL_2026-10-17.md), runners and [20 October development result](NATIVE_DEVELOPMENT_2026-10-20.md) remain unchanged. Validation results were already known before this proposal; a richer experiment is a post-development proposal, not a preregistered extension of the original run. Do not rerun the original development command into a different directory, append features to its sealed inputs, or use confirmation to repair its weak validation performance. This audit performed no fitting, predictions, scoring, target association, confirmation-outcome analysis or application activation.

## One-order aggregation and leakage controls

These are proposed deterministic definitions to freeze in a new protocol, not code changes:

- Assert unique order/customer-per-order/product/seller keys and unique `(order_id, order_item_id)` pairs. Filter the allowed order set before enriching items; validate many-to-one lookups. Aggregate enriched item rows before joining to orders, then assert exactly one row and one evaluation weight per original order. Missing item data must not silently remove orders or become genuine zero-valued baskets.
- Count actual item records; do not use `max(order_item_id)` as a quantity shortcut. Sum each item row's price and freight once, subject to the amount-semantics gate. Repeated product rows represent separate item records; distinct-product count is a separate quantity. Do not sum a repeated order-level total after a join.
- Geography: use customer state and the set of **distinct** seller states. For the narrow proposal encode seller state as the sole state if all sellers share it, otherwise a fixed `MULTIPLE` token; encode `UNKNOWN` for incomplete lookup. `all_sellers_same_state_as_customer` requires every required state to be known; unknown is not false. This avoids arbitrary first-seller selection and variable-length identity lists. Train-only category encoding must handle unseen states/tokens. A region variant uses the same rule after a pinned lookup.
- A later product variant should predefine category-set handling (for example sole category versus fixed `MULTIPLE`/`UNKNOWN`) and sum item-record product weight and `length * height * width`. The latter is a sum of catalogue bounding-box volumes, not a packed shipment dimension. Preserve missing-item counts/fractions; never silently sum missing measurements as zero. Numeric imputation/scaling and learned category vocabularies fit only on training rows. Do not derive bins, clipping limits, category merges or transformations from validation/confirmation distributions.
- A later distance variant should freeze a ZIP lookup reduction before modelling (for example coordinate medians after deterministic validity checks), establish source reference-date limitations and prohibit joining the raw geolocation table into model rows. For multiple sellers, a declared maximum over distinct seller endpoints could represent the farthest approximate origin; missing endpoints require a separate indicator and explicit incomplete-coverage policy. It is straight-line ZIP-proxy distance, not road distance, route length, delivery GPS or travel time. No external geocoding or address upload is needed or authorized.

## Historical seller performance: required temporal contract

For prediction order `o` at purchase time `P_o`, history may include only another order `h` with `P_h < P_o` and a qualifying outcome **known strictly before** `P_o`. Self-orders and simultaneous purchases are excluded. A prior purchase alone is not an observed outcome. `D_h < P_o` is necessary for recorded delivery to precede prediction, but is not evidence of contemporaneous ingestion; the snapshot assumption must remain explicit. If the new protocol retains the conservative maturity requirement, require `date(E_h) + 31 days <= P_o` as well. Never convert still-pending, cancelled, missing-delivery or other noncore orders into on-time/late labels, or retrospectively relabel them using later information.

**Training rows need their own temporal history.** An aggregate computed at the 1 January 2018 training origin and attached to earlier training rows contains future outcomes relative to those purchases and may contain the row's own label. Excluding self alone is insufficient. Build each training feature chronologically with outcomes released only when their declared availability/maturity conditions pass. Offline leave-one-out or shuffled cross-validation target means do not solve this time leakage.

Distinguish two future experiment designs:

1. **Frozen training-origin history for evaluation:** training features use row-specific prior history; validation and later evaluation use a seller table frozen at the training origin from admissible training-origin evidence. No validation outcome updates the table. This is the narrower proposal if history is later approved. Cold-start support counts and missing-history flags are necessary, with any fallback/smoothing prior itself available at the relevant time; a full-training prevalence fallback must not leak future labels into early training rows.
2. **Rolling history:** each evaluation prediction can update from earlier orders only after outcomes actually become available. This changes the information regime and may allow earlier validation outcomes as inputs to later predictions. It needs a separate prequential protocol, append-only availability events, defined maturity and frozen model/threshold choices; it is not equivalent to frozen-history validation. Do not silently include purchases from the original unused windows. Final-snapshot retrospective replay still cannot prove real-time availability.

Each completed order contributes at most one observation to a seller history even if it has several items from that seller. For multi-seller orders the available outcome is the whole order's delivery, not that seller's shipment outcome: any history is a **seller-associated order-lateness proxy**, not causal seller fault. Aggregate seller histories to the prediction order using a fixed distinct-seller rule, report support and cold-start status, and never expose raw seller identity as a predictor. No histories or historical outcome statistics were computed in this audit.

## Narrow next experiment and gates

The smallest recommended next **proposal** is `B4 + I3 + G3`: retain the four frozen calendar/lead-time inputs; add item-record count, sum of item prices and sum of item freight values; add customer state, sole-or-multiple seller-state token and all-sellers-same-state relation. If the location-as-of assumptions cannot be accepted, `B4 + I3` is the smaller fallback. Regions are an alternative geography representation to select in advance. Do not run both options and select the favourable result without counting them as separate development candidates. Product, distance, extra counts and seller history remain outside this narrow proposal.

Before any implementation or fitting:

1. Record a new dated/versioned availability decision accepting or rejecting each snapshot assumption, verify amount/measurement semantics where obtainable, and freeze exact aggregation/missingness/category rules. Acceptance permits a conditional benchmark; it does not manufacture historical proof.
2. Fix the new fit budget, RF/DT/LR comparisons and Dummy diagnostic, selection/threshold rule, resource limits, exposure ledger and a suitable independent evaluation strategy. Do not alter the old sealed selection. Reusing its validation window is further development on exposed data and must be disclosed. Preserve the original unscored confirmation for its existing locked experiment unless an explicit amended decision establishes its role and handles multiple-experiment selection; do not treat it as a freely reusable tuning pool.
3. Verify order-grain, train-only preprocessing, missing/unseen handling and temporal-history boundaries with synthetic cases before any real-source fit. Freeze source/code/protocol manifests and obtain a separate execution instruction. Test self, same-time, pending and future-outcome exclusion if history is ever included.

The report's RF/DT/LR comparison and primary Low/Medium/High task remain required. Olist still lacks complete failed-attempt, proof and terminal-failure histories. Extra predictors cannot turn this completed-order binary target into that primary three-class evaluation, establish operational acceptance or justify activation in IILAS.
